## Caveats
- Toy models, not the project's real netlist/firmware: B1 uses a random 120-gate network standing in for the hop mapper; B4 uses synthetic Hamming-weight + Gaussian leakage (no real device, no jitter/desync); B9 assumes Gaussian skew and jitter and an attacker who cannot reduce his own jitter floor; B10 is white-box on a 4-feature moment classifier, perturbation is abstract L2 on symbols (not an over-the-air signal); B11 uses a synthetic channel-quality model.
- B1: k is capped at 20 (a larger k gives too few activatable candidates); detection of trigger probabilities below ~1e-4 is not measured. Toggle detector has a nonzero false-positive load on the golden netlist (about 9 rare nets per set).
- B11: the "edit" case is detected 0.967 only because 1 of 30 random edits was a no-op (replaced a digit with itself).
- B10: adversarial-training result uses a short schedule (150 clean + 150 PGD epochs); stronger training would shift the numbers.
- Tests compare stored results with closed forms or known values (AES S-box, sqrt(2/(2+sigma^2)), 1-(1-p)^N, ln10/p) and with orderings that would break if the model were wrong; they do not rerun the long scripts.
- B14: all cycle counts are assumptions, not measurements of the project's MCU; the queue is a single FIFO of 8 with the structural check treated as ISR-level work (cost counted, queue not used). Per-source rate limiting was not evaluated because source identity is spoofable; the 2^-16 pre-tag passes one forgery in 65536 to the full MAC.
- B21: the spectral-mask rule is modelled as a minimum separation between consecutive hops; max-entropy over naive redraw gains only ~0.01 bit, so the real finding is the cost of the constraint itself and the occupancy imbalance. The adversary model is a first-order Markov learner (accuracy ~1/35 for all three chains, so it barely separates them; entropy rate is the discriminating measure).
- Tests total: 47 checks (python3 test_lab9.py also runs test_b14_dos.py and test_b21_maxent.py).
