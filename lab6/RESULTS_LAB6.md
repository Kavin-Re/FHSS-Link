## Results and caveats, per idea
**R1 Bandit poisoning.** Model: 13 channels, p_jam 0.95, p_clean 0.01. For 1500 slots the jammer jams each of the 10 good channels with probability 0.5 per slot, leaving 3 bait channels clean, then jams the bait. Phase-2 delivery over 1500 slots (8 seeds): uniform 0.77, Thompson 0.90 (clean 0.98), Thompson + 10% floor 0.81, Thompson + discount 0.98 0.96, both 0.95. In the first 300 slots: Thompson 0.52 (0.50 in the scorecard run), floor-only 0.21, discount 0.94. Finding: the damage is a transient (banked trust takes hundreds of failures to unwind) and the jammer pays about 7500 channel-slots of jamming for it. A uniform floor alone did NOT help and made the first 300 slots worse; my explanation (not isolated by an experiment) is that a well-sampled mediocre channel stops being drawn by Thompson, so the floor removes the natural exploration of uncertain channels. Discounting is the fix. Caveat: one poisoning strategy, i.i.d. fading-free channel, jammer oblivious to the learner. The "detect inconsistent feedback" lesson from the idea list was NOT implemented.

**R2 Learning-rate attack.** Jammer relocates a 3-channel jam set every L slots. Delivery for gamma=1 runs 0.80 (L=10) to 0.99 (static); gamma=0.98 runs 0.85 to 0.97; uniform 0.77 everywhere. Negative result: no resonance near L = memory length and no L pushes any learner below uniform; the harm is largest for the fastest relocation. A surprise-triggered forgetting rule (switch-detect) lifts gamma=1 at L=10 from 0.80 to 0.86; it never fires for gamma=0.98 (identical numbers). Caveat: the jammer is oblivious to the learner's choices; a reactive jammer was not tested. The "damage 0.18 HOLDS" in the scorecard is measured against the static-jammer delivery, while 61 to 81% of the learner's advantage over uniform is stripped (shown in the evidence column); both views are honest and measure different goals.

**R3 Control-plane jamming.** Beacon every 20 slots, sync lost after D slots without one, out-of-sync receiver can only guess among R rendezvous channels. Jamming 1 data channel costs availability 0.99 to 0.917 (about 1/13) at 100% duty. Jamming only the beacon on the rendezvous channel (5% duty, one channel) drops the naive design (R=1, D=60) to 0.135. R=3,D=60: 0.904; R=5,D=100: 0.988; R=5,D=200: 0.990. Caveats: the jammer knows the beacon period and the rendezvous set; a fixed beacon period is itself a timing leak (idea 5), so beacon-time randomisation would be the next step but is not modelled; the cost of more rendezvous channels (slower cold-start resync, about 6 beacon periods at R=5 under attack) is only visible in the availability number.

**R4 Time-pushing.** 2-state Kalman filter (offset, skew), beacon 1 per second, jitter 3 us, 30 us guard; ramp of r us per second from t=150 s (12 seeds). Guard violated after 290 s (0.1 ppm), 149 s (0.2), 60 s (0.5), 32 s (1), 19.5 s (2), 11 s (5), always about guard/r. The standard 3-sigma innovation gate sees little below 2 ppm (hits equal the no-attack 2.2 per run at 0.1 to 0.5 ppm; at 1 ppm 3.0 per run, slightly visible). Skew-rate check (skew estimate moves more than 0.8 ppm within 60 s; threshold set from the no-attack maximum 0.71 ppm over 30 seeds; false alarms 0 of 12 runs, i.e. a 95 % upper bound of about 22 %): no detection at 0.1 and 0.2 ppm, 17% at 0.5, 92% at 1 ppm but later than the violation (39 s vs 32 s), wins only at 2 ppm and above (17.5 s vs 19.5 s, marginal) and 5 ppm (9 s vs 11 s). Honest result: this defence is not enough against pushes of 1 ppm and below; stronger options (faster beacons, a better reference oscillator, cross-checking two independent time sources) are untested. Caveat: filter model copied in spirit from lab2/clock_kalman.py, not identical; the threshold was tuned on the same noise model it is tested on.

**R5 PRNG graveyard.** Attacker knows the algorithm, sees only past channel numbers. LCG with 24-bit state (a=1140671485, c=12820163), hop=(x>>8) mod 13: candidate filtering over all 2^24 states leaves one state after 8 hops (5 trials; this 8 is for the LCG24 generator, not the 16-bit LFSR schedule, which needs 8 or 9, see docs/RESULTS.md; all future hops predicted). xorshift32 on a 16-channel plan (4 bits per hop): GF(2) linear solve, 9 hops (5 trials). MT19937: untemper 624 consecutive 32-bit outputs, clone, then 2000 further outputs match Python's own generator. HMAC-SHA256: best generic predictor 0.084 vs chance 0.077 over 3000 hops (about 1.5 standard errors, consistent with chance; the test only requires accuracy below 0.12). Cost in pure Python (indicative, varies per run): LCG about 0.1 us/hop, xorshift 0.7 (0.66 in the stored log), MT 0.07, HMAC 2 to 5. Caveats: the 24-bit LCG is a toy size (a 32-bit state needs a smarter or compiled search, not shown); the xorshift attack needs a power-of-two channel count since mod 13 breaks linearity; MT needs the full 32-bit outputs, with only 4 bits per hop it would need roughly 5000 hops and GF(2) algebra that is NOT implemented; HMAC "unbroken" only means this simple predictor fails, not a proof.

**R6 Key-from-air entropy drain.** Same family as sim/plkg.py, re-implemented. Fixed threshold calibrated in the normal environment; noise 0.5 sigma_f. Normal: H=1.0, disagreement 0.20, secret rate 0.273 bits/sample. Holding the environment still (alpha = fade std ratio) leaves A's bit entropy at 1.0 but disagreement rises (0.33 at alpha 0.5, 0.49 at 0.1): a denial of key agreement, not a leak. Adding a 0.3 sigma mean nudge also biases the bits (H 0.85, min-entropy 0.47 bit per bit), so an attacker who guesses the likelier bit knows about 68 of 128 key bits (60.2 bits of entropy remain). The monitor (abort below 0.15 secret bits/sample from observed H and disagreement) accepts the normal case and rejects every alpha at or below 0.5 (alpha 0.7 still passes at 0.16). Caveat: independent Gaussian samples, no eavesdropper channel model, monitor threshold chosen by me; the monitor turns the leak into a denial of service.

**R7 Myopic jammer.** Jammer detects the active channel with accuracy a after latency d (in dwell units) and jams the rest of the dwell; packet lost if overlap > FEC tolerance theta=0.25. a=1: delivery 0.050 for d up to 0.75 minus; once d > 1-theta the follower falls back to blind guessing: delivery 0.918 for a=0.25, 0.5 and 1.0 alike. a=0.5 at d=0: 0.484. Past-channel predictor on i.i.d. uniform hopping: 0.0773 vs chance 0.0769. The lesson is a latency budget: hop (or sub-hop) fast enough that detection latency exceeds (1-theta) of the dwell, as lab5 fast-hop explores. Caveat: Monte Carlo and closed form agree within 0.01 by construction (the MC mainly validates the code); the model is a threshold model, with no partial-erasure decoder curve.

**R8 Scorecard.** Eight rows (R1 to R7 plus the timing-leak idea 5 copied from sim/redteam_ladder.py), two configurations. Damage per row is the fraction of the defender's own goal lost (row-specific, see exp8_scorecard.py); verdict HOLDS below 0.25, DEGRADED below 0.6, else BROKEN. Baseline 0.48, 0.18, 0.85, 1.00, 1.00, 0.53, 1.00, 0.94 (R1 to R7, timing leak); hardened 0.03, 0.14, 0.00, 0.67, 0.02, 0.00, 0.09, 0.00. R4 stays BROKEN for hardened, deliberately kept as a visible limitation. Caveat: the "hardened" configuration is the lesson of each experiment, so the scorecard is a summary of these experiments and not an independent validation; the attacker cost tiers are my labels.
